# Decision context and assumptions

Use at B02 to establish the premises of the decision. Read the current brief, delivered DK and relevant current evidence or prior results. Preserve exact output/source references and source limitations.

Build separate records for binding constraints, user preferences, verified facts, source assertions, working assumptions and unknowns. State why each matters to the decision and its applicability, conditions and exceptions. Do not convert a preference into a universal requirement or a historical statement into a current fact. Identify assumptions inherited from the initial idea rather than independently established.

At Program level use the published strategic brief and its business/organizational consequences. At Scope level expose outcome boundaries, obligations and dependencies. Retrieve finer detail only for a question that materially depends on it. A broad-search hit is a candidate source; inspect its authority and applicability before using it. An empty DK base is an explicit starting condition, not a reason to invent background knowledge.

For each material source distinguish freshness, authority, provenance, confidence and allowed use. A current secondary summary and a stale authoritative statement are different limitations. Label generated or memory-derived pointers and find the original when a conclusion depends on it. Preserve known contradictions, negative evidence and restricted-source gaps without exposing protected content.

Write `decision-context.md` with the premise table, source basis, existing decisions, current-state boundary, dependencies, assumptions and unknowns, conflicting constraints, access/freshness limits and next proof. Set `context_basis` and `assumptions_and_limits`.

Return to B01 if the evidence shows that the decision has been framed around a different goal or owner. Otherwise carry material unknowns to criteria/evidence review. Do not decide the winner or expand into unrestricted research in the context phase.
