# Operations knowledge: responsibilities, cadence and measurable contracts

Apply General and this method to operating models and organizational procedures. Distinguish an agreed operating rule, a proposed model and an observation of actual practice. Publishing a responsibility does not grant tool access, assign a person externally or prove adoption.

Evidence: identify operating purpose, accountable owner, source authority, roles/responsibilities, cadence, handoffs/escalation and status semantics. Pin metric/SLA/SLO definitions, units, windows, thresholds and signal sources. Separate the definition of a measure from a measured result. Record exception handling, ownership gaps and observations of practice with their time/scope.

Create: express a usable bounded operating model: trigger/input, responsibility, expected output, handoff, status meaning, cadence, metrics and escalation/exception route. Preserve unresolved ownership explicitly. Do not invent a team, queue, dashboard, policy or approval requirement simply because the template has a field. An absent requirement is an explicit documented limit, not fabricated completeness.

Revise: show changes of responsibility, cadence, status, handoff, metric semantics or exception handling. Identify affected teams/processes and changed expectations. Historical measurements retain the definitions under which they were collected; do not relabel them under a new SLO. A process document update is not evidence of operational adoption.

Revalidate: use new suitable evidence that the unchanged model, authority, owners, cadence and signal sources remain applicable. Where actual practice is asserted, use dated practice evidence. A copied role list or advanced review date is insufficient. Changed semantics, ownership obligations or escalation paths require revise.

Supersede: compare successor coverage of purpose, responsibilities, triggers, handoffs, status semantics, metrics and exceptions. Enumerate replaced consumer bindings and transition ownership. Do not silently discard unresolved incidents, queues, commitments or old metric interpretation. A successor model can be accepted while its operational rollout remains a separate follow-up.

Retract: withdraw the scoped rule/model from current guidance and identify unsupported handoffs or ownership gaps it leaves. Preserve reason and history. Retraction does not cancel external commitments, close incidents or notify people automatically.

Erase: inventory owned operating-model text, role examples, meeting/source fragments, metric snapshots and derived procedure copies. Respect shared and independently retained records; do not erase unrelated operational history through an overbroad source reference. Preserve only the agreed minimal tombstone and suppression information.

Impact and terminal checks: distinguish knowledge acceptance, actual practice, incident follow-up, recurring review and external communications. Record concrete owners for unresolved handoffs and gaps. Capture a lesson only with its scope and source; a single incident does not automatically establish a universal operating policy. The publication lifecycle performs no notification or recurring scheduler action.
