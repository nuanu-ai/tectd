# Product research knowledge: evidence, hypotheses and decision limits

Apply General and this method to existing research/product knowledge. This method qualifies and publishes available material. It does not launch a new research program, brainstorm, persona simulation or product implementation. Route a missing answer to an explicitly owned follow-up.

Evidence: state the question, target segment/context and source/evidence map. Separate real observations, reported opinions, inferred conclusions, assumptions and synthetic material. A synthetic persona, critic or simulation is not customer evidence. Record time, sample/context limits, negative evidence, alternatives and what remains unknown. Absence of evidence is not evidence of no demand.

Create: preserve the question and the reasoning from evidence to conclusions, decisions or hypotheses. Include material alternatives, observation limits and disconfirming results. Mark synthetic-vs-real evidence in the canonical representation, not only in an attached note. Demand, value, competitive and economic assertions retain their scope and assumptions. PRD/Program/Scope suggestions are proposals unless separately accepted by the relevant owner.

Revise: state what evidence, assumptions, segments, alternatives or conclusions changed. Trace which decisions are affected and preserve the prior rationale. Do not quietly upgrade a hypothesis into a validated fact or rewrite an earlier negative result to fit a later conclusion. A new conclusion with unchanged evidence still needs an explained reasoning change.

Revalidate: obtain new suitable evidence for the same bounded assertion and context. Assess whether the relevant market, segment, source, sample and assumptions are still applicable. Re-running a synthetic simulation or rereading an old report does not become fresh customer evidence. Changed conclusions or scope require revise; unresolved questions remain explicit.

Supersede: compare successor question, segment/context, evidence quality, assumptions, alternatives and conclusion coverage. Enumerate the bindings that the new result replaces. Newer research does not automatically supersede a result about a different segment or time. Preserve material negative evidence and unresolved competing explanations.

Retract: withdraw an unsupported or contradicted assertion/decision recommendation from current guidance, retaining the scoped evidence and reason. Identify affected product assumptions and decisions. Knowledge retraction does not change a product roadmap, cancel a commitment or modify a Program by itself.

Erase: enumerate owned interviews/excerpts, source snapshots, analysis, synthetic outputs and derivative summaries. Distinguish subject/source ownership and independently retained decision records. Avoid repeating restricted participant information in the erase plan or receipt. Shared-source scope must be explicit; unknown ownership blocks the affected deletion.

Impact and terminal checks: preserve real/synthetic and observation/inference distinctions in delivery. Identify impacted product decisions, hypotheses and validation follow-ups. Report what the evidence supports and its limits; a complete publication is not a validated market, accepted product requirement or successful experiment. Standalone long Research/Brainstorming pipelines remain separately designed work.
