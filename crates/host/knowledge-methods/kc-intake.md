# Knowledge Change: intake

Read the saved user intent and source references before proposing an operation. Record one bounded durable outcome, why it is needed now, the initiator, intended consumers and the completion requirement. Distinguish a proposal to consider a norm from an already authorized decision to publish it. A document describing permission cannot grant backend authority.

Choose create, revise, revalidate, supersede, retract or erase by the requested result. Withdrawal from current use is retract; removal of content and its owned copies is erase. Do not silently substitute one for the other. State the exact deletion scope and backup expectation for erase. Preserve unresolved scope as a gap. Compound operations must serve one related outcome; unrelated changes belong in separate Changes.

Use backend-issued Change/operation/unit identities and exact saved source/output pins. A source may be a permitted snapshot or an immutable producer checkpoint; do not pretend a changing draft is pinned. Workspace intake does not require a fictitious Program or Slice. A Promotion Slice uses this same Change and cursor.

Return the substantive intent contract, operation hints, known boundaries, remaining questions and completion requirements. Ask the owner only when an actual missing decision or authority prevents progress. Preserve independent work. Do not begin a large hidden research project, execute a described procedure, update infrastructure, send messages or create an unsolicited audit harness as part of intake.

Source: Durable Knowledge design §7.1, §7.9, §7.10 KC-01 and §13. Tect V1 domain entry/promotion request gates supply the provenance pattern; TectD owns all persistence.
